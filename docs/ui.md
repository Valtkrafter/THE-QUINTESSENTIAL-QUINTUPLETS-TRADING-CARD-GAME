# UI structure (Stage 2)

## Screens

| Route | Screen | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `#/` | Binder (`ui/binder`) | Every owned copy as a flat picture; search, filter (character, rarity, finish, graded or not), sort (number, rarity, finish, newest, score); a graded copy carries its score and a ring in the colour of its band |
| `#/grading` | Grading (`ui/grading`) | Send a loose card in (the fee and the price ladder first, then the reveal) and look at your slabs and your scores; see "Grading" below |
| `#/battle` | Battle (`ui/battle`) | The lobby (pick a rival and a deck; a first-time player is offered the animated explanation) and, once a battle starts, the table; see "Battle" below |
| `#/decks`, `#/decks/new`, `#/decks/<id>`, `#/decks/template/<id>` | Decks (`ui/decks`) | The player's decks and the loaner decks; the deck builder |
| `#/shop` | Shop (`ui/shop`) | The Kiosk (eight single cards a day) and the buyer of spare cards; see `docs/packs-und-wirtschaft.md` section 8 |
| `#/packs` | Packs (`ui/packs`) | The packs as cards (Booster, Select, Premium, and the Halloween Wish while it runs): price, how often a good card comes, a guarantee meter; open 1 or 5, the daily bonus, the last packs |
| `#/packs/odds/<id>` | Odds (`ui/packs`) | For one pack: what you get at what chance (every slot, and the pack as a whole), the guarantees in packs, the banner rules and how many packs a good card takes on average. Made from the same data as the draw, so it cannot disagree with it |
| `#/dex` | Card-Dex (`ui/dex`) | All 79 designs, owned ones in colour, missing ones dim; progress overall and per character |
| `#/card/<uid>` | Inspect (`ui/inspect`) | One copy in 3D (drag to turn, double-click to flip) with the wear of its hidden marks, facts, showcase toggle, arrow keys to the neighbours; a loose copy has a "Grade it" button, a graded copy sits in its 3D slab and shows its score, marks and value, with "Sell slab" |
| `#/showcase` | Showcase (`ui/showcase`) | Up to 6 copies on display; picker dialog |
| `#/settings` | Settings (`ui/settings`) | Frame style, graphics level, idle motion, save export/import/reset, development grants (dev build only) |
| `#/lab` | Holo Lab (`ui/lab`) | The old playground, kept as a dev tool |

Routing is hash-based (`wouter/use-hash-location`), so the game also works from a static host or inside Tauri without server rules. The two 3D screens are loaded on demand, so the binder starts without three.js.

## Why thumbnails

A binder with 80 cards cannot run 80 WebGL views. Each card face is painted once with the same 2D code as the 3D card (`cardface/thumbnails.ts`), shrunk to 360×504, kept as a WebP and shown as an `<img>`. Painting is queued and starts only when a tile comes near the screen. WebP is used because JPEG encoding was about 25 times slower in the browser pane (1 s against 40 ms per card). Next to the picture the thumbnail keeps two foil masks: a small alpha mask for a CSS shimmer (`ui/components/CardFace.module.css`, the fallback) and the raw mask the shader reads.

## Live foil tiles

Cards with a finish run the real foil shader of the 3D card, in every grid (binder, Card-Dex, showcase). `holo/tileRenderer.ts` owns ONE hidden WebGL renderer for the whole page: every frame it draws the cards that are on screen with `createFrontShader()` (the same shader and the same tilt and light maths as the inspect view, see `holo/tileMath.ts`) and copies each picture into that card's own 2D canvas. The page then clips, scrolls and stacks the cards like any other element, and the number of WebGL views stays at one. `ui/hooks/useHoloTile.ts` connects a `CardFace` to it (visibility through an `IntersectionObserver`, pointer tilt, canvas size) and loads the renderer on demand, so the binder still starts without three.js and shows flat pictures first.

- Only visible cards cost anything. Cards that nobody touches are redrawn about 40 times a second (24 on "normal" graphics, once for a still card), the card under the pointer in every frame; at most 10 pictures per frame, and when frames get slow the idle cards are redrawn less often. A card that was off screen for 4 s gives its textures back, after 20 s its canvas; with no card left the renderer is thrown away after 15 s.
- The CSS shimmer stays as the fallback: low graphics, no WebGL, a graphics chip reset, or a picture that has not been drawn yet. `CardFace` gets `live={false}` where a picture must stay flat (inside the pack show, the poster under the inspect view).
- The plain finish stays a flat picture with the CSS light show on hover: it has no foil to draw.
- Development check: in the browser, `await import(<url of tileRenderer in the Network list>)` then `holoTileStats()` gives cards served, pictures drawn and the time spent drawing.

## Battle (Stage 4)

`ui/battle/Lobby.tsx` lists the five rivals (portrait, level, the type they play and what it is weak to, what a win pays) and the decks the player can take: their own (a deck that is not ready says why) and the loaner decks. Somebody who has never played a battle sees a "New to battles?" card first, which opens the animated explanation (below).

**The table** (`Board.tsx`) is built like the animated explanation on purpose, so what was explained is what is on the screen: the six steps of a turn on the left, the rival's side above and yours below, six Memory dots for each player, your hand and the free Heart on the right, and under it all one line that says what to do next.

- **A fixed stage.** The table is drawn on a stage with its own measures that is scaled to the window (`layout.ts`): a wide one (1280 × 660) and a tall one (420 × 884) for a phone held upright or a narrow window. Both stages are fitted into the window as a whole; the tall one only when that keeps it readable (a scale of 0.8 or more, which most phones held upright reach), otherwise it keeps the width of the window and scrolls. So a card always stands where the design puts it, on a laptop and on a big screen alike. `layout.test.ts` checks that nothing overlaps and everything is on the stage. The caption line and the menu button are outside the stage.
- **The coach** (`coach.ts`, pure and tested). `turnGuide` works out from the battle and `legalActions` which of the six steps (draw, Heart, Bench, evolve, Supporter, attack) are done, can be done now or cannot, so the list on the left never suggests something the rules refuse. `setupGuide` does the same for getting ready (a Basic in front, the Bench, Ready). `captionFor` gives the sentence under the table and the buttons that belong to it (Ready, End turn). Every sentence has a test.
- **Picking things up.** A card of the hand, the free Heart or a Character is picked up (`Board.tsx` keeps what is in the hand of the player) and everything it can go to glows. A Basic goes to the Bench with one click. The free Heart is a token on the right: click it, then a glowing Character. The moves of the Active Character are buttons in the moves panel, with the damage they would really do to the enemy; the panel next to the rival's Active Character says what it is weak to and what that means for yours (`FoePanel` in `stageParts.tsx`). Questions of the engine are answered on the table (the possible targets glow) or, for cards, in a sheet (`Prompts.tsx`).
- **What happened.** Floating numbers (`floaters.ts`), a ghost of a Character that was knocked out, a pop when a Memory is won, and sound (below). The text log is in the battle menu, and as a hidden `role="log"` for screen readers.
- **The shell.** While a table is open, the app shell hides the side menu (`AppShell`, `immersive`). The menu button opens "Battle menu": back to the battle, how to play, watch how it works, the log, give up, leave (the battle waits).
- **Reading cards.** The card under the pointer is shown large in the panel on the right of the wide table. On a phone a card is looked at by pressing and holding it (`useHold.ts`: about half a second with a finger or pen, a mouse keeps hovering); it opens large in a dialog. The Characters of the enemy answer to a press and hold as well, they only do nothing else.
- **Tested, pure parts:** `viewModel.ts` (what can be done with a card or a Character, whose turn it feels like), `coach.ts`, `layout.ts`, `floaters.ts`, `choices.ts` (which decks can be taken), `sounds.ts`.

**The animated explanation** is a page of its own, `public/battle-demo.html` with `battle-demo.js`: six scenes (setup, a turn step by step, damage and weakness, Prime cards, a Halloween card, victory) with pause, back, next and speed buttons. `Explainer.tsx` shows it in a window, in a frame that may only run its own script. It is offered in the lobby (first time and in the header), at the top of "How to play" and in the battle menu. It uses the real numbers of real cards (Nino, Ichika, Itsuki, Fūtarō); if a rule or one of those cards changes, the scenes need the same change. It keeps its own script file, so the strict security policy of the Windows app needs no exception for it.

**Sound.** Every step of the engine also makes sound (`ui/battle/sounds.ts` turns the events into cues, `audio/battleAudio.ts` makes them with Tone.js, like the pack show): a card is placed or evolves, a Heart is given, an attack swishes and lands (heavier with more damage, bright for a weakness, dull for a resistance, a metal ping when blocked), a heal, a swap, a knock-out (bigger for a Prime), the start of your turn, and the end of the battle. The volume is the one setting for all sound; Tone.js loads when the first battle starts.

`state/battleSession.ts` runs the battle: it holds the engine's state and the log, lets the rival act one action at a time with a short pause, does the rival's setup at once and writes the result into the save game when the battle ends (`battle/rewards.ts`). A battle is not saved: closing the page ends it. In the development server `window.__battle` gives the session, the roster and a few helpers for looking at a situation in the console.

## Decks (Stage 4)

`ui/decks/DecksScreen.tsx` lists the player's decks (edit, copy, delete) and the loaner decks (use as a template). `DeckEditor.tsx` is the deck builder: the player's cards on the left (search, characters or supporters; a card that cannot be added says why on hover), the deck on the right with one row per design and minus and plus, the rules check in plain words, and the card under the pointer enlarged. A template keeps the cards the player does not own and marks them. Decks may be saved unfinished. The rules of changing a deck are pure and tested (`editing.ts`).

## Grading (Stage 6)

**The rules** are pure code in `domain/grading.ts`. Every copy has four marks that show as wear in the 3D view (rolled when the copy was printed, see `domain/ownership.ts`). At the desk the graders look closer than anybody can: on every mark they may find something more (`gradeMarks`: the more wear shows, the likelier it is, and a mark that shows nothing is not safe either), and the score is the average of the marks they give, rounded down to half a point (9.75 and up is a 10). The fee depends on the rarity and is paid whatever the score is. The value of the case is the price of the card times what the score adds (`gradeMultiplier`): a 9 doubles it, a 10 multiplies it by ten, and below an 8.5 the case is worth less than the loose card was. So grading is a gamble that only pays for the cards that look best. What the desk tells the player is worked out by the same code that does the grading: the chance of every score (`gradeChances`, exact, a convolution of the four marks, not a sample), how the card looks to the eye (`eyeCheck`) and a verdict (`gradeOutlook`: great, good, risky or bad bet). `submitForGrading`, `sellSlab`, `populationReport` and the chances are all tested. The numbers are in `docs/packs-und-wirtschaft.md` section 9.

**The wear** is what makes the marks visible before a card is graded. `holo/shaders/cardFront.frag.glsl` takes four wear values (`holo/wear.ts` turns a mark into 0 for a perfect one and 1 for the worst): an off-centre print (a white strip on one side), whitened corners and edges, scratches that flash when the light slides along them, and a dull haze. `wearSeed` (from the copy's uid) makes every worn copy wear differently. The inspect view uses it; grids stay clean.

**The slab** is built in `holo/slabShape.ts` and `holo/Slab.tsx` as a child of the card, so it turns, tilts and flips with it: a dark insert with a window for the card, the paper label above it (`holo/slabLabel.ts` paints it on a 2D canvas: logo, name, the four marks and the score in a box, in three palettes: gold for a 10, silver for 9 to 9.5, paper for the rest) and a shell of clear plastic (`shaders/slab.frag.glsl`: a faint tint, a glow along the edges, a glint of the light, a soft reflection that slides over it). No glass and no environment map, so it costs one cheap pass on every graphics card. Without WebGL the flat `ui/grading/SlabFlat.tsx` shows the same label over the card.

**The screens.** `GradingScreen.tsx` has two tabs. "Send a card" lists the loose cards (search, sort by the best bet or by the cleanest look, a filter for clean-looking ones), each with how it looks to the eye. On a wide screen the desk (`GradeDesk.tsx`) sits beside the list: the card in 3D under a lamp ("look at it in 3D first"), how it looks to the eye, the four things the graders check, the chance of every score next to what the card would be worth in its case, a verdict in plain words and the fee. On a narrow screen a tap opens the same desk as a question (`GradeFlow.tsx`, also opened from the inspect view): the desk, the fee, "Yes, send it in". The save is written first, then `Ceremony.tsx` plays the reveal as a small state machine: the card lies under a lamp and is looked over four times (centering, corners, edges, surface); after each look a dial fills with the mark, then the marks are added up, the score is stamped and the card is sealed in its slab, which turns in 3D (the 3D viewer loads on demand, `SlabFlat` is the fallback). It can be skipped at any moment (click, Space, Enter, Escape or the button) and it shows everything at once for people who asked their system for reduced motion. "Your slabs" (`Slabs.tsx`) shows the population report (how many slabs have each score), the total value and every slab, best first.

## State

- `state/gameStore.ts`: the save in memory, loading, writing (latest change wins, failures are reported), damaged-save handling, fallback to memory when the browser refuses storage.
- `state/preferences.ts`: per-device settings (frame style, graphics, idle motion), separate from the save so an exported save does not carry them.
- `state/app.ts`: the one game and the one set of preferences of the page, plus `useGame` / `useSave` / `usePreferences`.
- Save version 2 added the showcase, version 3 the daily bonus, the pack history and the guarantee memories (`domain/save.ts`, migrations tested).

## Graphics level

Three levels (High, Medium, Low in Settings) change how sharp and how busy the 3D and foil drawing is: the pixel ratio of the 3D card (2, 1.5, 1), how often the live foil tiles are redrawn (40, 24 per second; none on Low, where the CSS shimmer stays), and, in the pack show, the size of the glowing background (which also shrinks by itself when the show runs slowly). The first time the game starts on a device it looks at the graphics chip once (`state/gpu.ts`) and picks the starting level: Low without WebGL or with a software renderer, Medium on a phone or tablet and on the graphics chip built into an Intel processor (UHD, HD, Iris, Xe), High otherwise (NVIDIA, AMD, Apple, Intel Arc, or a browser that does not say). The choice is saved with the other settings, so it is made only once, and the player can change it at any time.

## Accessibility

Every screen is checked by axe-core in the end-to-end tests (`e2e/accessibility.spec.ts`; serious and critical problems fail the build). What is in place: a skip link and a title for every page, landmarks (menu, main, side panels), a focus ring on everything that takes focus, names for every button and picture, native dialogs (focus stays inside, Escape closes) and a Tab trap in the full-screen grading reveal, a `role="log"` for the battle log, bigger controls on touch screens (`pointer: coarse`), motion switched off for people who asked their system for less of it, `lang="ja"` on Japanese names, and text colours that keep the contrast limit on every surface. Not done yet: a full keyboard play of the battle table (it works with Tab, Enter and Space, but has no shortcuts) and a read-aloud description of what the 3D card looks like.

## Theme "Dämmerung"

All colours, spacing, corners, shadows, type sizes and motion are variables in `ui/theme/tokens.css`; nothing else holds a value. Surfaces are a calm blue-grey, actions use a soft periwinkle, a warm horizon peach is used sparingly, and each Heart type has a pastel (`--affinity-heart` ... `--affinity-study`). Change a value there and every screen follows.

## Text

Every sentence is in `ui/text.ts`, in English and in plain words (the game words are listed in `docs/glossary.md`); the names of the rarities, finishes and stages live next to their data. The language of numbers and dates is one constant in `ui/locale.ts`.

## The pull show (Stage 3)

- `scene/pull/director.ts` is the timeline as pure code (phases tear, intro, flight, impact, ceremony, enter, flip, show, exit, summary; modes full, short, off). `runner.ts` plays it and tells the scene (every frame), the screen (on phase changes) and the sound (events).
- The cinematic before the cards is modelled on a gacha reference (Wuthering Waves), rebuilt from scratch in shaders, nothing ripped: the camera falls into a storm (`storm.frag.glsl`), a needle of light with a long trail dives into a gate, a dark hole ringed by turbulent clouds in the colour of the best card (`gate.frag.glsl`), with speed lines and thin orbits (`streaks.frag.glsl`), then a radial-blur dive and a white-out with a cross-star and veins (`burst.frag.glsl`). Art Rare and better then get a ceremony on a black screen (`ceremony.frag.glsl`): a thin line, a jagged spike and three, four or five stars that light up one by one (the five stars of an Ultra Rare wear the Heart colours). Packs with a Special Art Rare or Ultra Rare also weave the five Heart-coloured threads round the needle, which hangs for a moment before it plunges.
- `scene/pull/fx/PullFx.ts` owns everything that glows (storm, needle, trail, threads, gate, white-out, ceremony, shock waves, rays, particles). The background goes through a bloom, a radial blur and colour fringes (`useGlowCompositor.ts`, `composite.frag.glsl`); the card scene is drawn on top without it, so the card looks exactly like in the inspect view. Every shader is compiled before the show starts, and a show that runs too slowly draws its background at a smaller size (`speedMeter.ts`).
- **The tear.** Before the show the pack lies on the table and the player opens it: a swipe along the top, or a tap (or the Space key). `ui/pull/PackTear.tsx` draws three clipped copies of the pack art (the rest of the pack, the strip and the flap that folds back) and `tearController.ts` follows the finger; `tearShape.ts` is the pure geometry (the ragged edge, the angle of the flap, how far the drag has come, where a release ends up) and is tested. Letting go past the middle (or a tap) finishes the tear with a white flash, and the flash starts the show. The tear is a phase of its own in the director: it waits for the player, every pack of a batch of five starts with one, and with the pack animation off there is none. The sound is the cue `tear` in `audio/pullAudio.ts`.
- `audio/pullAudio.ts` makes the sound with Tone.js (no sound files). It is loaded when the first pack is opened.
- Development helpers (only in the dev server): Settings > Development has "Preview animation" (plays the show with made-up cards for any best rarity), the pack simulator and money buttons. In the browser console `window.__pullRunner` is the running show; setting `window.__pullFreeze = true` stops time and `window.__pullAdvance = seconds` steps it, for looking at single frames.
